AI for workshop operations · German Experts, Abu Dhabi · 2024 to now

Taking the typing out of vehicle check-in

Every visit to the workshop starts with someone copying a vehicle registration card into the CRM. That was the first thing I automated, and it grew into a small set of AI tools around the service desk.

Problem
Registration details were typed into the CRM by hand at every check-in, at the busiest point of the day.
What I built
An OCR reader for UAE registration cards, a number-plate recognition API, a photo view check and AI-written inspection reports.
Outcome
Registration data reaches SQL Server and the CRM without anyone typing it, and inspection reports come out consistent and ready for the customer.

Context

German Experts is a multi-brand luxury and EV service group with branches in Abu Dhabi and Dubai. The customer and vehicle record in the CRM drives everything that follows a visit: the job, the invoice, any contract, the report.

The registration card (the Mulkiya) is the most reliable source for that record, so it was the obvious place to start.

The problem

  • Staff read each card and typed the details in. A wrong digit in a VIN or plate number travels all the way to invoices and contracts.
  • Inspection reports depended on whoever wrote them, so the quality varied.
  • Inspection photos sometimes showed the wrong side of the car, and nobody noticed until later.

What I did

  • Built the registration-card reader in Python on an open-source vision model. It corrects commonly confused digits, decodes the VIN and writes structured data into SQL Server and the CRM.
  • Built a number-plate recognition API in .NET, hosted on IIS, that reads plates from all seven emirates.
  • Built a view check that confirms an inspection photo shows the side of the vehicle it is supposed to.
  • Added AI-generated inspection reports to the CRM, written from technician inputs, with a secure page the customer can open.
  • Set up self-hosted agents that rewrite technician and advisor notes into clear, professional text.

How I approached it

  • Start with the step that repeats most. Registration capture happens on every visit, so it pays back fastest.
  • Fill the fields people already use. The reader writes into the CRM's own customer and vehicle records. Nobody had to learn a new screen.
  • Keep the models in-house. They run on our own server, so customer data stays on the premises.
  • Don't trust the model blindly. The reader checks digits and decodes the VIN before anything is saved.

How it fits together

Where it landed

  • Registration data entry at check-in is automated.
  • Number-plate recognition covers all seven emirates.
  • Inspection reports are consistent and ready to send to the customer.
  • The models run on our own hardware, not a third-party service.

Built with Python, Ollama, Qwen2.5-VL, OCR and computer vision, C# / .NET, IIS, SQL Server
